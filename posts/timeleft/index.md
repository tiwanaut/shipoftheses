# Timeleft

## TL;DR

- Timeleft is a social platform that seats six strangers at a partner restaurant on a fixed weekly schedule, matched on a personality questionnaire. It runs dinners, drinks and group runs in more than 200 cities across 50+ countries. Till date, it has seated over 3 million guests and offers women-only tables in more than 50 cities. ARR reached >$20m against 150,000 monthly participants roughly 20 months after launch; the company's disclosed funding is $7m across two rounds.
- I propose that Airbnb should acquire Timeleft and fold it in as 'Interactions', a third product line alongside Experiences and Services. Travel would remain Airbnb's core use case, but Interactions would stay bookable at home with no trip attached, on the same basis that Experiences already run for residents in 650 cities. On an assumed $25m of current ARR I would expect a price of $100m–$150m, or 4x–6x revenue, structured 70% stock and 30% cash. Maxime Barbier, the founder of Timeleft, would join Airbnb as 'VP of Interactions'.

## The Company

### Background

- Timeleft was incorporated in Paris in 2020 by Maxime Barbier and Adrien De Oliveira. The original concept matched users on shared bucket-list activities so they would go and complete them together; it unfortunately failed to stick. However, users proved willing to meet strangers in lower-stakes settings such as a group meal, and the weekly stranger-dinner format launched in May 2023.
- How it works: six strangers are matched at a partner restaurant, matched based on factors including interests, decision-making style, age band, and more. Participants see their tablemates' industries and nationalities the evening before, and the venue on the morning of the dinner. Timeleft handles the venue, the group and the time; participants settle their own bill at the restaurant.
- More recently, the formats have exteneded beyond the Wednesday 'once-a-week' dinner. Timeleft now runs drinks in casual bar settings, group runs, women-only Tuesday tables in more than 50 cities, and an optional after-party where several tables from the same night converge on a nearby bar.

### Financials

- Timeleft is private and discloses selectively. The $25m ARR figure for 2026 used throughout is an assumption, and the derived figures below are based on my own estimates.
- The last company-stated position is ~$20m of ARR against 150,000 monthly participants. That implies roughly $14 of revenue per participant-month, consistent with a low-priced membership bought by members attending two or more times a month. 150,000 monthly participants equates to approximately 35,000 seats a week, or around 5,800 tables of six. At $30–$40 of spend a head, Timeleft steers something in the order of $1.1m–$1.4m of weekly revenue into partner restaurants, or $55m–$70m a year. Most notably: the company's revenue reached $1m MRR a year-and-a-half after launch, whilst the company still ran on low-code tooling.

### Funding

- CB Insights records $7m raised over two rounds, the most recent a $7m Series A in 2024; the company lists Redstone, Kima Ventures, FJ Labs, Play Ventures and SIDE Angels as amongst its investors.
- Based on the funding amount and an estimated $25m of ARR for FY2026, I would expect the founders and employees hold the large majority of the equity. A 70% stock component is therefore payable to holders who can accept illiquid consideration, whilst the cash portion of the acquisition provides investors with liquidity.

## Thesis

- Airbnb reported Q2 2026 revenue of $3.6bn, up 17%, and gross booking value of $27.2bn, up 16%. Nights and seats booked grew 10%, net income was $816m and adjusted EBITDA was $1.3bn. Nights booked on the app grew 23% and now account for 64% of total nights, up from 59%. First-time booker growth accelerated to 11%, the highest in four years, with Gen Z the fastest-growing cohort within it.
- Airbnb has published the conversion data behind its entry-point strategy. Nearly 25% of guests new to Airbnb who book an experience go on to book a stay or a service. Roughly one in three experience bookers book a stay within 90 days, and around 55% of guests who book a hotel return to book a home. Experiences supply grew nearly 80% year on year in Q2 2026, on 1,000 additions in the quarter.
- $100m–$150m sits between an eighth and a fifth of a single quarter's net income. Airbnb committed $250m in 2025 to relaunching Experiences alone. Per my reckoning, a purchase at this size falls below the spend already committed to the neighbouring line.
- However, the acquisition may not be accretive. Experiences launched in 2016, and the product relaunched in May 2025 with a $250m allocation. The company has spent quite a while attempting to move the company past short-term rentals, and one could argue that it may simply not be possible. However, I believe the Timeleft acquisition coupled with the launch of Interactions could position Airbnb as a hospitality platform, and not just a travel-focused one. In turn, this could ensure the product becomes stickier and enables more recurring use. 
- I propose a purchase price of $100m–$150m, 4x–6x on an assumed $25m of ARR, structured 70% stock and 30% cash. The stock weighting reflects a cap table on which founders and employees hold most of the equity, and binds them to the outcome.
- Following the acquisition, Maxime Barbier would join Airbnb as VP of Interactions, with Interactions launching as a third line alongside Experiences and Services. It should be monetised as a membership rather than through the 20% Experiences deduction. Travel is the core use case, but much like Experiences, the the product stays bookable at home for users who are not travelling at all.
