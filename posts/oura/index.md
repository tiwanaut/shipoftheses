# Oura

## TL;DR

- Oura has sold more than 5.5 million rings since 2015 and doubled revenue in each of the last three years to approximately $1bn in 2025. This is projected to exceed $1.5bn in 2026, with CEO Tom Hale indicating an upper case closer to $2bn. It was valued at $11bn in an October 2025 Series E and filed confidentially for a US listing in May 2026.
- However, I believe Oura should not proceed with the listing, but instead be acquired by Apple. Whilst building a ring sits within Apple's engineering reach—given it has filed ring-related patents since 2019 and designs its own silicon and optical sensors—it would be unable to match Oura's competitive foothold. If at all, I would expect an attempt at this to cost 1-2 years and substantial marketing expenditure to approach. Should this acquisition manifest, I would expect a price of $20bn—roughly 13x 2026 revenue—structured 60% cash and 40% stock.

## The Company

### Background

- Oura was founded in Oulu, Finland in 2013 by Petteri Lahtela, Kari Kivelä and Markku Koskela, three engineers drawn from the Nokia and Polar ecosystem in northern Finland. They worked from the proposition that the finger produces a stronger and cleaner pulse signal than the wrist, and therefore supports more accurate heart-rate variability and respiratory measurement.
- The product is a titanium ring that tracks more than fifty physiological signals continuously, paired with an application and a paid membership that converts those signals into sleep, readiness and recovery scores. At the time of the IPO filing, the company reported over five million paid members, a fourfold increase over two years, and that 80% of members renew after their first year.
- Until now, Oura has been an acquirer itself four times. It acquired Proxy in 2023, Veri and Sparta Science in 2024, and Doublepoint in March 2026. Hale has described the direction as a cloud of wearables in which multiple sensors feed a single analytics engine.
- Oura's positioning within the enterprise segment is less visible but strategically valuable. It describes the US Department of Defense as its largest enterprise customer, has worked with the DoD since 2019, and was awarded a contract of more than $96m in October 2024 to supply rings and analytics to the Defense Health Agency, with the platform deployed through Palantir's FedStart programme.

## Market positioning

- Samsung launched the Galaxy Ring in 2024 at $399 with no subscription, and its successor has been delayed to early 2027 following weak sales and the ITC dispute, which leaves the premium end of the category without a credible second option through late 2026. Ultrahuman and RingConn were both excluded from the US market by the ITC in October 2025, with RingConn subsequently settling and taking a royalty-bearing licence from Oura, and Circular having licensed a year earlier, so the low-cost tier now competes in the United States either on Oura's terms or not at all.
- Given its dominance in hardware, Apple is difficult to exclude from the category. It holds 2.5 billion active devices, an installed base which presumably overlaps heavily with Oura's own customers, ring-related patent filings going back several years, and a newly-appointed CEO from hardware engineering. Mark Gurman reported in October 2024 that Apple had no plans for a ring, precisely because of cannibalisation risks with the Apple Watch, and Hale said the same in November 2024. The market has therefore _anticipated_ and rejected an Apple ring, but nobody has ruled out an acquisition of the kind I am proposing.

## Exit

- Oura submitted a draft registration statement to the SEC on 21 May 2026, with Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Allen & Co and Jefferies reported as working on the offering. Given it holds category leadership at approximately three quarters of global ring shipments, I do not argue that a listing would fail. Instead, I believe an acquisition by Apple would be the better outcome for Oura and its investors, for Apple, and for consumers.
- There are, however, two objections. First, Oura is a genuine category creator. Second, a company growing at 50% today has strong long-run potential. Fitbit serves as the partial precedent. It listed in June 2015 as the leader in fitness tracking, lost share to Apple and Samsung over the following four years, and was sold to Google for $2.1bn in a transaction announced in November 2019. Whilst Oura holds patents Fitbit did not and sells into a category Apple has not yet entered, the parallel only goes so far. Nonetheless, the underlying narrative holds.

## Thesis

- Apple could build its own ring device(s), but I would expect it to need 1-2 years to approach Oura's position. This is both a build-to-buy question and one regarding whether a global company like Apple possesses the go-to-market leverage to dominate the ring market in a compressed timeframe.
- Although it has been previously suggested that an Oura acquisition would cannibalise the Apple Watch, I feel it would actually complement the product—especially for users seeking more discrete options. Moreover, the argument in favour of integrating Oura's ring suite becomes more compelling given Oura's string of acquisitions enhancing the sensors underpinning its technology. 
- An Apple<>Oura acquisition would be structured 60% cash and 40% stock, allowing for an early and meaningful liquidity event for Oura's institutional investors, and a bold move aligning with John Ternus's new role as Apple CEO, as well as a hint to a new product-led era for the company.
