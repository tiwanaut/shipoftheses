# Monzo

## TL;DR

- Monzo is the UK's largest digital bank, with over 15 million customers, FY2026 revenue of £1.7bn (up 39%), adjusted pre-tax profit of £172.6m and customer deposits of £25.7bn. It has appointed Morgan Stanley ahead of a mooted IPO at £6bn–£10bn, though no venue, prospectus or date has been confirmed, and the board replaced a CEO who wanted to list sooner.
- Whilst Monzo's product is near-identical to Revolut's, Revolut's business differs greatly in scale. It has 70 million customers, £4.5bn of revenue at a 38% pre-tax margin and a $75bn valuation, and has out-built Monzo on trading, FX, crypto and multi-currency. However, Monzo's edge is depth rather than breadth. It earns roughly £183 per active customer against an implied ~£66 at Revolut, because it is often seen and used as a primary bank rather than a secondary spending account.
- A public listing—presumably on the LSE—is unadvisable, as the LSE remains deeply unpopular with high-growth issuers. However, a US listing such as the Nasdaq would ask American investors to buy a bank that shut its US operation in April 2026 and has no presence or brand there. In my view, Monzo should acquire Payhawk to extend Business Banking into mid-market and enterprise, then sell to JPMorgan at roughly 1.5x its latest private marks, approximately $9bn–$12bn (£7bn–£9bn) in cash and stock, in a window of 2028 into 2029.

## The Company

### Background

- Monzo was founded in 2015 by Tom Blomfield, Jonas Templestein, Jason Bates, Paul Rippon, and Gary Dolman, who met while working at Starling Bank. It set a crowdfunding record by raising £1m on Crowdcube in 96 seconds in 2016, and it received its full UK banking licence in 2017; it is now the country's seventh-largest bank by customer numbers.
- In December 2025, Monzo became the first digital bank to secure a full European banking licence from the Central Bank of Ireland and the European Central Bank, launching in Ireland in April 2026 off a waitlist of 100,000, with Spain named as the next market. In the same week it announced its first-ever acquisition, the digital mortgage broker Habito, completed in April 2026. Unfortunately, it announced its US departure in late, closing an operation that had run through a partner bank since 2022 and concentrating investment on the UK and Europe.
- TS Anil stepped down as Group CEO in February 2026 after what Sifted reported were disagreements with the board over listing timing, with Anil favouring an earlier IPO and directors preferring to grow the valuation first. His successor, Diana Layfield, formerly of Google and Standard Chartered, has told the FT she is "not in a hurry" to list.

### Financials

- FY2026 has been the company's strongest yet: revenue rose 39% to £1.7bn, gross profit exceeded £1bn for the first time, statutory pre-tax profit grew 44% to £87.3m and adjusted pre-tax profit rose 20% to £172.6m, marking a third consecutive profitable year. The revenue base now spans four income streams: current account balances, borrowing, payments and wealth. Additionally, customer deposits grew 55% to £25.7bn, its lending book grew 42%, and the bank added a record 3 million customers, 79% of whom reportedly joined through word of mouth. Business Banking had a standout year, with customers up 45% to over 905,000, equivalent to one in seven UK businesses, and now contributes 14% of total revenue.
- The confirmed valuation figures factor in the $2.70bn Series F in 2019, through a pandemic down round at $1.68bn in 2020, to $4.5bn in December 2021, $5.4bn in March 2024 with Alphabet's CapitalG, and a secondary sale at roughly $5.9bn in October 2024 backed by GIC and StepStone. By mid-2026, Nasdaq Private Market's estimate implied a share price around $18.71, consistent with a secondary-implied value approaching $8bn.

## Market positioning

- Monzo's core product maps almost one-to-one onto Revolut's: an app-first current account, cards, savings pots, budgeting, lending, subscriptions and a growing investment offer. Revolut's 2025 results showed revenue of £4.5bn (+46%), 68.3 million retail customers across roughly 40 markets, and customer balances of $67.5bn. It counts around 13 million customers in the UK alone, which makes Revolut nearly as large in Monzo's home market as Monzo itself, while being roughly ten times larger everywhere else.
- On the other hand, Monzo's advantage is depth. Its £183 of revenue per active customer compares with an implied figure of roughly £66 per retail customer at Revolut. It is a valuable franchise, though a smaller and slower one than Revolut will inevitably take to public markets—currently speculated at around $100bn.

## Exit

- Whichever exit route Monzo takes, I believe it should first acquire Payhawk. Monzo Business is the fastest-growing part of the company and the most strategically incomplete, since its products serve sole traders and small companies well while offering little to enterprise finance teams, and building that capability organically means dedicated enterprise product development and an enterprise sales and support motion.
- Payhawk boasts an expansive feature suite: corporate cards, expense management, AP automation to mid-market and enterprise finance teams, and its own EMI and FCA authorisations. It reported annual recurring revenue of €39.5m for 2024, and Bloomberg reported in January 2026 that it was in early talks to raise more than $100m at a valuation approaching $2bn.

## Thesis

- I propose—and predict—Monzo be sold to a strategic acquirer rather than listing on the public markets. A buyer gets incredible distribution and a strong community-led neobank brand; this cannot be replicated organically at any sensible cost. A strategic acquirer would gain over 15 million customers acquired mostly through word of mouth, >£25bn of deposits growing at more than 50% YoY, a full UK banking licence, and, post-Payhawk, a business bank spanning sole trader to enterprise. I believe JPMorgan would be an excellent option to acquire Monzo.
- JPMorgan launched Chase UK in September 2021 with the stated ambition of joining the top few UK banks, and nearly five years on has reached roughly 2.5 million customers and £22bn of deposits, about a sixth of Monzo's customer base. Notably, Monzo added more customers in FY2026 alone than Chase UK has accumulated in total. JPMorgan has also shown the acquisition behaviour this thesis requires, buying Nutmeg in 2021 for a reported £700m, integrating it into Chase UK and retiring the brand in November 2025 in favour of J.P. Morgan Personal Investing. It holds 40% of Brazil's C6 Bank and launched Chase in Germany in May 2026, which makes Monzo's Irish licence and its Ireland and Spain launches an accelerant for the next steps of its implied growth strategy.
- The transaction is an acquisition of a regulated, deposit-taking bank, so PRA and FCA change-of-control approval would be key, and consideration would likely be cash and stock. At 1.5x the most recent marks, that is approximately $9bn–$12bn (£7bn–£9bn), or roughly 4x–5.5x FY2026 revenue for a profitable bank compounding revenue at 39%.
- If the acquisition were to occur, it may be worth retiring the Monzo brand. Its identity was built in deliberate opposition to the institutions that would buy it, and maintaining the Monzo brand may conflate the new positioning post-acquisition. Nonetheless, it gains Monzo's customers, deposits, lending book.
