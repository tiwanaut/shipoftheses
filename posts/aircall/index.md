# Aircall

## TL;DR

- Aircall is a cloud voice and customer communications platform used by more than 23,000 businesses across over 100 countries, with ARR above $200m as of June 2026, up from $175m in April 2025 and $100m in June 2022. It is profitable, has raised $226m in total, and was last marked at just over $1bn in a June 2021 Series D led by Goldman Sachs Asset Management.
- The company spent 2026 rebuilding around agentic voice, buying Vogent in May for its voice AI pipeline and Piper AI in June for revenue intelligence, then opening its AI to outside developers over MCP on 1 September. The launch partners were Attio, HubSpot, HelpScout, Front and Calendly, all of which sit on the non-Salesforce side of the SMB stack.
- If Salesforce were to acquire Aircall, I expect it would be for $1.6bn–$2.0bn in cash (7x–9x ARR). Salesforce built Agentforce Contact Center for the enterprise and bought Fin for the SMB digital channel, but it has no self-serve phone system for small and mid-sized customers and no numbers of its own outside North America. Aircall could be the Salesforce-native voice product for that segment.

## The Company

### Background

- Aircall was founded in Paris in 2014 by Olivier Pailhès, Jonathan Anguelov, Pierre-Baptiste Béchu and Xavier Durand, on the premise that a business phone system should be software that plugs into the CRM. It is now headquartered in New York with engineering concentrated in Paris, and runs around 600 to 700 staff across nine global offices.
- The product unifies voice, SMS and WhatsApp in a single workspace with more than 250 native integrations, alongside AI Assist for live coaching and summaries, AI Voice Agent from April 2025 and AI Messaging Agents from May 2026. Entry tiers start at three licences and the custom tier at 25, which puts the sales motion in self-serve rather than enterprise procurement.
- The Salesforce partnership dates to 2017 and was deepened in August 2025 with a native integration into Service Cloud Voice covering nearly 50,000 users. Aircall sells it as a distinct line, Aircall for Salesforce Voice, and gates the Salesforce CTI integration to its Professional tier.

### Financials

- The ARR path is unusually well disclosed for a private company: $1m in March 2016, $10m in mid-2018, $100m in June 2022 on 13,000 customers, $130m by October 2023, $175m in April 2025 at around 25% growth with stated profitability, and above $200m by June 2026. I would estimate current ARR at ~$225m, reflecting a growth of 20%–25%.
- Revenue doubled between June 2022 and June 2026 while the customer count rose 77%, from 13,000 to 23,000, so implied revenue per customer moved only from roughly $7,700 to roughly $8,700. Growth is carried by new logos more than by expansion inside accounts, which leaves a wide, shallow and under-monetised base. Extracting more from it requires either a much larger AI attach or an owner with adjacent products to sell into it.
- Aircall has raised just over $225m in total—around one year of current revenue—and has taken no primary capital since February 2022. Nothing forces a sale on operating grounds; the pressure sits on the cap table.

## Market positioning

- The Communications as a Service (CaaS) category splits primarily in three ways: Enterprise CCaaS (ECaaS), Unified CaaS (UCaaS), and CRM-attached—voice for small and mid-sized teams—to which Aircall belongs.
- Within the CaaS category, the publicly listed companies are cheap stocks with materially limited growth. Companies like Five9, RingCentral, and 8x8. I would consider Genesys an exception as it also possesses customer service solutions within its product suite.
- I feel Aircall offers a uniquely differentiated positioning which competes on scale, deployment speed, ICP, and a favourable pricing model which is a per-seat telephony licence. Salesforce notably stopped selling new Sales Dialer licences in its Spring '26 release while shipping native voice inside Agentforce Contact Center.

## Exit

- Salesforce guided FY27 revenue to $46.1bn to $46.4bn, with Agentforce ARR past $1.5bn in Q2 FY27, up more than 240% year on year, against an FY30 target of $63bn. The acquisition cadence supports the argument: Informatica at $8bn closed in November 2025, $750m into Genesys, Contentful closed on 1 September 2026 at a price The Information reported at $1bn to $1.5bn, and Fin (formerly Intercom) at $3.6bn signed in June 2026.
- Salesforce has already built the enterprise contact centre. Agentforce Voice went generally available in October 2025 with Amazon Connect, Five9, Genesys, NICE and Vonage as partners, and Agentforce Contact Center followed in March 2026 with native telephony on Hyperforce and Bandwidth supplying the voice infrastructure, after a fifteen-month build staffed by hires from all five CCaaS Magic Quadrant leaders. It shipped as a US add-on to Agentforce Service, in English for the US and UK, sold to organisations that run a contact centre function.
- What that build does not reach is Aircall's segment or its geography. Three-licence customers buying without an implementation partner need a low-touch motion Salesforce does not have and would struggle to run alongside the enterprise sale, and numbers in more than 100 countries take carrier arrangements and local registrations that cost calendar time rather than engineering time.
- Fin brings roughly 30,000 mostly SMB customers and an agent resolving queries across chat, email, WhatsApp, SMS, phone and Slack, with the Fin line at around $100m ARR growing 350% inside a $400m total, and it reaches the phone channel through third-party integrations, Aircall among them. Buying Aircall converts what is effectively a "rented" channel into an owned one for exactly the substantial customer size Fin serves.

## Thesis

- I propose that Salesforce acquire Aircall for $1.6bn–$2.0bn in cash (7x–9x ARR). Fin at $3.6bn on $400m of ARR sets the ceiling at 9x, though its $100m line growing 350% is something Aircall does not have, and Contentful's reported $1bn to $1.5bn against a $3bn 2021 valuation sets the discount applied to a post-2021 era asset that matured and slowed following the pandemic. Aircall sits between both, with profitability and a live Salesforce distribution position pulling it towards the upper half band.
- Should Salesforce not acquire Aircall, HubSpot could be a potential challenger—especially given HubSpot Ventures on Aircall's cap table since 2022. Moreover, Aircall has been named its 2025 Co-selling Partner of the Year, a Premier partner, and an Essential App for Sales for the fourth year running. For this to be actualised, it would reflect an acquisition significantly larger than any of its previous; its largest disclosed acquisition was Clearbit at a $146.8m purchase price. It is interesting, however, that in August 2026, KeyBanc named HubSpot itself among the software companies most likely to be acquired.
