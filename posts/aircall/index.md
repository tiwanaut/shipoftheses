# Aircall

## TL;DR

- Aircall is a cloud voice and customer communications platform used by more than 23,000 businesses across over 100 countries, with company-stated ARR above $200m as of June 2026, up from $175m in April 2025 and $100m in June 2022. It has raised $226m in total, has been profitable since 2025, and was last marked at just over $1bn in a June 2021 Series D led by Goldman Sachs Asset Management.
- The company has spent 2026 rebuilding itself around agentic voice, acquiring Vogent in May for its voice AI pipeline and Piper AI in June for revenue intelligence, then opening its AI to outside developers over MCP on 1 September. The launch partners for that ecosystem were Attio, HubSpot, HelpScout, Front and Calendly, all of which sit on the non-Salesforce side of the SMB stack.
- I expect Aircall to be acquired by Salesforce for $1.6bn to $2.0bn in cash, roughly 7x to 9x ARR, in a window running from late 2027 into 2028. Salesforce built Agentforce Contact Center for the enterprise and bought Fin for the SMB digital channel; it has neither built nor bought a self-serve phone system for small and mid-sized customers with numbers in more than 100 countries. Aircall is already the Salesforce-native voice product for that segment. A listing is the weaker route, since at the 2.0x to 2.4x revenue on which Five9 and RingCentral currently trade, Aircall would price below its 2021 private mark.

## The Company

### Background

- Aircall was founded in Paris in 2014 inside the eFounders studio, now Hexa, by Olivier Pailhès, Jonathan Anguelov, Pierre-Baptiste Béchu and Xavier Durand, on the premise that a business phone system should be software that plugs into the CRM rather than infrastructure that sits beside it. The company is now headquartered in New York with engineering still concentrated in Paris, and around 600 to 700 staff across nine offices including San Francisco, Seattle, London, Berlin, Madrid and Sydney.
- The product unifies voice, SMS and WhatsApp in one workspace and connects to more than 250 business tools natively, with AI Assist for real-time coaching and post-call summaries, AI Voice Agent launched in April 2025, and AI Messaging Agents launched in May 2026. Entry tiers start at a three-licence minimum and the custom tier at 25, which places the sales motion in self-serve and low-touch rather than enterprise procurement.
- Leadership has turned over twice. Pailhès took the company to $100m ARR, Michael Haske was appointed CEO in September 2022 and stood down, at which point Pailhès resumed the role, and Scott Chancellor was appointed in December 2023 from Humu, having previously been chief product and technology officer at Apptio and spent seven years at AWS. He arrived with a stated target of profitability within twelve months and a mandate to expand in the US, and brought Tom Chen as CPO and Alan Talanoa as CTO in March 2024.
- The Salesforce relationship dates to 2017 and was deepened in August 2025 with a native integration into Salesforce Service Cloud Voice, which Aircall described as serving nearly 50,000 users. Aircall sells that capability as a distinct line, Aircall for Salesforce Voice, and gates the Salesforce CTI integration to its Professional tier.

### Financials

- The ARR path is unusually well documented for a private company: $1m in March 2016, $10m in mid-2018, $100m in June 2022 on 13,000 customers, $130m by October 2023, $175m in April 2025 at around 25% growth with stated profitability, and above $200m by June 2026. On that trajectory I would estimate current ARR at $215m to $230m, still growing at roughly 20% to 25%.
- Revenue doubled between June 2022 and June 2026 while the customer count rose 77%, from 13,000 to 23,000. Implied revenue per customer therefore moved from about $7,700 to about $8,700, a gain of roughly 13% over four years. Growth is being carried by new logos more than by expansion inside accounts, which leaves the installed base wide, shallow and under-monetised. Monetising it further requires either a much larger AI attach or an owner that can sell adjacent products into it.
- Profitability changes the exit calculus. Aircall has raised $226m in total, which is around one year of current revenue, has taken no primary capital since February 2022, and does not need a financing event. Nothing forces a sale on timing; the pressure comes from the cap table rather than the P&L.

### Funding

- Total funding of $226m across eight rounds: a $2.75m round led by Balderton in 2016, $29m in 2018 led by Draper Esprit (now Molten), $65m in May 2020 led by DTCP with Adams Street and Swisscom, and the $120m Series D in June 2021 led by Goldman Sachs Asset Management at a valuation above $1bn, extended in February 2022 with HubSpot Ventures joining.
- Goldman Sachs Asset Management, DTCP and Adams Street are therefore five years into a 2021-vintage growth position in a company that has grown into profitability rather than reaccelerated. That cohort needs a realisation, and the realisation available in public markets is poor.

## Market positioning

- The category splits three ways. Enterprise CCaaS is Genesys, at nearly $2.1bn ARR when it took $1.5bn from Salesforce and ServiceNow in July 2025, alongside NICE, Five9 at $1.260bn to $1.272bn of guided 2026 revenue, and Talkdesk. UCaaS is RingCentral, guiding to $2.635bn to $2.646bn for 2026, plus 8x8 and Zoom Phone. Aircall sits in the third tier, CRM-attached voice for small and mid-sized teams, against Dialpad, Quo (formerly OpenPhone), JustCall and CloudTalk.
- The listed comparables are cheap and slow. Five9 carries a market capitalisation of roughly $2.5bn on about $1.27bn of revenue growing 9%; RingCentral roughly $6.2bn on $2.64bn growing 5%. That is 2.0x to 2.4x sales. RingCentral also disclosed in Q1 2026 that ARR from customers using at least one paid AI product had passed 10% of total ARR, having doubled year on year, so the AI transition in this category is being priced as a defence of the existing base rather than as a new growth engine.
- Aircall does not compete on scale, being roughly a sixth of Five9. It competes on where it is installed: 250 integrations, deployment in days, and a buyer who is a sales or support manager inside a CRM rather than a contact centre operations function. That position is also its exposure. A per-seat telephony licence is the part of the customer stack most easily absorbed by whoever owns the record of the customer, and Salesforce stopped selling new Sales Dialer licences in its Spring '26 release while shipping native voice inside Agentforce Contact Center.

## Strategic rationale

- Salesforce guided FY27 revenue to $46.1bn to $46.4bn, up 11% to 12%, on Q2 FY27 revenue of $11.35bn, cRPO of $33.5bn up 14%, and a non-GAAP operating margin of 34.1%, against an FY30 target of $63bn. Agentforce ARR passed $1.5bn in Q2 FY27, up more than 240% year on year. The acquisition cadence is heavy: Informatica at $8bn closed in November 2025, $750m into Genesys in July 2025, Qualified and Regrello, Contentful closed on 1 September 2026 at a price The Information reported at $1bn to $1.5bn, and Fin, formerly Intercom, at $3.6bn signed in June 2026 and closing in Q4 FY27.
- Salesforce has built the enterprise contact centre itself. Agentforce Voice reached general availability on 21 October 2025 with Amazon Connect, Five9, Genesys, NICE and Vonage as partners, and Agentforce Contact Center followed at Enterprise Connect in March 2026 with native telephony running on Hyperforce and Bandwidth supplying the underlying voice and messaging infrastructure. Kishan Chetan, who runs Agentforce Service, described a fifteen-month build staffed by hires from all five CCaaS Magic Quadrant leaders.
- That build leaves Salesforce short in two places. Agentforce Contact Center went generally available as an add-on to Agentforce Service in the US, with voice in English for the US and UK, sold into organisations that run an operations function. Aircall's 23,000 accounts start at three licences and buy without an implementation partner. Salesforce has no low-touch voice motion, and constructing one would compete internally with the enterprise sale it has just spent fifteen months building.
- Geography is the second gap, and it consumes calendar time rather than engineering time. Aircall provisions numbers in more than 100 countries, with the carrier arrangements and local registrations that implies. Salesforce partnered with Bandwidth rather than assemble that itself, which is a reasonable read on how it values owning the plumbing against owning the workflow above it.
- Fin makes the fit sharper rather than redundant. Fin brings roughly 30,000 mostly SMB and mid-market customers and an AI agent that resolves queries across chat, email, WhatsApp, SMS, phone and Slack, with the Fin agent line at around $100m ARR growing 350% inside a $400m total. Fin reaches the phone channel partly through third-party integrations, Aircall among them. Buying Aircall converts a rented channel into an owned one for exactly the customer size Fin serves.
- On timing, I would weight the defensive case heavily. Aircall's ecosystem release on 1 September 2026 went live with Attio, HubSpot, HelpScout, Front and Calendly, and its AI Actions run against Shopify, HubSpot and Zendesk. Aircall was HubSpot's 2025 Co-selling Partner of the Year and is a Premier partner and an Essential App for Sales for the fourth consecutive year. Every quarter Aircall spends becoming the connective voice product for the non-Salesforce SMB stack raises both the acquisition price and the probability that someone else pays it.

## Synergies

- Most of the revenue case is positional. Aircall's Salesforce integration already touches nearly 50,000 users, and that cohort converts first to Agentforce consumption. The larger prize is the remainder of the 23,000 accounts running HubSpot, Pipedrive or Zendesk, which become Starter and Pro Suite targets held by a vendor with a reason to call them.
- The consumption maths is real but small at this scale. Agentforce Service Agent launched at $2 per conversation and now runs largely on credits. Attaching $2,000 a year of agent consumption to a third of Aircall's accounts is roughly $15m of incremental ARR, immaterial against $46bn of revenue. The case rests on owning the channel that generates the conversations Salesforce meters.
- Cost synergies are limited and I would not underwrite them. Aircall is already profitable on around 700 staff, so there is no loss to absorb and little duplicated headcount worth removing beyond general and administrative functions and some overlapping carrier procurement against Salesforce's Bandwidth arrangement. Salesforce said Contentful would continue operating on its existing platform and support model, and I would expect the same treatment for Aircall's Paris engineering centre.

## Thesis

- Salesforce should acquire Aircall for $1.6bn to $2.0bn in cash, announced between the second half of 2027 and 2028. On my estimated $215m to $230m of ARR growing 20% to 25% with positive EBITDA, that is 7x to 9x, and the whole of it is a strategic premium: at the 2.0x to 2.4x sales on which Five9 and RingCentral trade, Aircall is a $450m to $550m company.
- Two recent Salesforce deals bracket the range. Fin went at $3.6bn on $400m of ARR, roughly 9x blended, but with a $100m line growing 350% that Aircall does not have, so 9x is Aircall's ceiling. Contentful went at a reported $1bn to $1.5bn against a $3bn mark set in 2021, which is the discount applied to a 2021-vintage private asset that matured rather than reaccelerated. Aircall sits between the two, with profitability and a live Salesforce distribution position pulling it towards the upper half.
- Consideration should be cash. Salesforce paid cash for Informatica, stated that the Fin transaction would not affect its capital return programme, and settled a $25bn accelerated share repurchase in October 2026 having taken delivery of 103 million shares. Issuing paper for a $2bn asset would run against that programme. I would expect meaningful retention packages for the Vogent and Piper engineering teams, whose technology Salesforce would otherwise be paying for twice.
- The timing follows Fin. That deal closes in Q4 FY27, roughly November 2026 to January 2027, and integrating 30,000 customers and a second agent architecture absorbs most of FY28. The voice gap becomes acute during the international rollout of Agentforce Contact Center over the same period, which is when the build-or-buy question on SMB telephony gets asked properly. Late 2027 is the earliest sensible approach.
- HubSpot holds the strongest competing claim, given HubSpot Ventures sits on the cap table from February 2022, Aircall is its co-selling partner of the year, and its CRM is the primary system of record for a large part of Aircall's base. For HubSpot to win, it would need to write a cheque an order of magnitude larger than anything it has done, its largest disclosed acquisition being Clearbit at a $146.8m purchase price per its 2023 10-K, and it would need to be buying capability rather than revenue it already partly monetises through the partnership. Neither condition holds. HubSpot was itself named by KeyBanc in August 2026 among the software names most likely to be acquired, which sits awkwardly with a $2bn purchase of an adjacency.
- A CCaaS or UCaaS buyer is ruled out on overlap and currency. Five9's entire market capitalisation is roughly $2.5bn and it appears on the same takeover list; RingCentral grows at 5% organically and already owns RingCX, so it would be paying a strategic premium for seats it competes for today. Consolidation inside the category buys the same customer twice at the category multiple.
- Microsoft covers both ends of this already, with Dynamics 365 Contact Center above and Teams Phone below, and Aircall's base is substantially composed of teams that chose against that stack. The acquired revenue would be substitution rather than extension.
- Private equity is a credible bidder on paper, since a profitable, recurring, roughly $220m ARR asset is a straightforward sponsor case. It wins only if both Salesforce and HubSpot pass, because a sponsor must clear the same price without synergies and refinance a business whose core licence is under pressure from the CRM owners.
- The arithmetic on a listing does not work. Aircall would go public into a comparable set trading at 2.0x to 2.4x sales, at a scale of roughly $220m of ARR that is thin for a US listing, and at a price below the mark its 2021 investors already hold. Pailhès said in October 2023 that an IPO became examinable at $200m of ARR; the company has now passed that number, and the market that would receive it has repriced the category in the interim.
- Regulatory friction is modest. There is no horizontal overlap in SMB voice, since Salesforce has no product in that tier, so clearance should be routine, and the work sits in change-of-control notifications for telecoms registrations across the jurisdictions where Aircall holds numbers. The French objection that surfaced over Contentful, a German-founded company passing under US jurisdiction, would recur for one of France's better-known technology exports, though Aircall has been US-headquartered for years and the objection is political rather than legal.
